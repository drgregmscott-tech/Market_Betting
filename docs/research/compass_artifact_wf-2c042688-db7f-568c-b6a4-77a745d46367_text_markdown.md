# Account-Limiting Policies of Fixed-Line Pick'em Platforms: PrizePicks, Underdog Fantasy, and DraftKings Pick6

## TL;DR
- **All three platforms reserve near-absolute contractual power to limit, refuse, cap, or close accounts "in their sole and absolute discretion, for any reason or no reason" — this is verified from primary-source Terms of Service. PrizePicks' ToS explicitly names "entry limits" as a tool it may apply; DraftKings Pick6's ToS explicitly bars bots, collusion/syndicates, and "advantage" play from non-public information; Underdog's ToS lets it cap entry fees based on "liability risk to our business."**
- **The widely-cited claim that PrizePicks demotes users to Flex-only and cuts max entry once win rate crosses ~55% over 200+ entries is single-sourced to one affiliate site (xclsvmedia.com) and could NOT be independently corroborated. Treat the specific numbers as unverified. What IS well-documented (BBB/Trustpilot first-hand complaints) is win-triggered account closures and withheld withdrawals at PrizePicks.**
- **The "vs.-the-house" pick'em model has drawn cease-and-desist action or rulings in more than a dozen states (per Legal Sports Report); PrizePicks and Underdog pivoted to peer-to-peer products (PrizePicks "Arena," Underdog "Pick 'Em Champions"), while DraftKings Pick6 — built as peer-to-peer on DraftKings' existing licenses from the start — sidesteps most of these bans.**

## Key Findings

1. **Contractual limiting power is essentially unlimited on all three platforms.** Each ToS grants sole-discretion rights to refuse entries, cap entry sizes, suspend, restrict, or terminate accounts, and withhold or claw back funds. This is verified primary-source fact.

2. **The specific PrizePicks "~55% / 200+ entries → Flex-only" threshold is not verifiable.** It originates from a single affiliate comparison article and appears nowhere in PrizePicks' own rules, nor in any independent Reddit/forum/X/journalistic source. Classify as **unverified — treat as unconfirmed.**

3. **First-hand PrizePicks complaints are numerous and specific, but describe a different mechanic than the threshold claim** — namely win-triggered account closure, restriction, and fund seizure/withheld withdrawals, not "account stays open but max entry drops." BBB's 2025 Gambling and Gaming Study found "SidePrize LLC, which does business as PrizePicks, had almost 500 complaints in the last three years" for withdrawal problems, account terminations, and seizing of funds.

4. **Reputationally, Underdog is considered the most winner-tolerant and DraftKings Pick6 structurally disincentivized from limiting** (peer-to-peer, fixed rake), but this rests largely on affiliate/industry commentary, not hard first-hand evidence. There is essentially no first-hand win-triggered limiting report for either Underdog Pick'em or DraftKings Pick6.

5. **State legal status differs sharply by platform** because of how each is licensed.

## Details

### 1. What the Terms of Service Actually Say

**PrizePicks (SidePrize LLC d/b/a PrizePicks; ToS last updated August 3, 2026).** The primary source is unambiguous:

- *Entry limits:* "PrizePicks may place limits on your account for legal, regulatory, responsible gaming, or other relevant purposes. These limits may include deposit limits … and entry limits. Limits will be set in PrizePicks' sole discretion and are subject to change." (Section 5)
- *Right to suspend/limit/close:* "PrizePicks shall be entitled to suspend, limit, deactivate or terminate your Account, if we determine, in our sole discretion, that you are … otherwise abusing or misusing the Services in any way whatsoever." (Section 5.1)
- *Discretion to restrict access generally:* "PrizePicks may also impose limits on certain features and services or restrict your access to parts or all of the Services without notice or liability." (Section 2)
- *Anti-syndicate / anti-proxy ("MultiAccounting"):* Users may not "direct, control … or otherwise use another User's Account … as a proxy … to make Contest entries, or to evade any restrictions, limitations or suspensions." Remedies include seizing all funds earned during the MultiAccounting period.
- *Fraud/integrity investigations:* PrizePicks may run "anti-fraud and/or integrity checks on playing patterns and Deposits prior to processing a withdrawal."
- Note: PrizePicks' ToS does NOT contain any published language describing a win-rate-based demotion from Power to Flex play. "Power" vs. "Flex" is presented purely as a user-chosen entry type with different payout structures.

**Underdog Fantasy (Underdog Sports, LLC; Fantasy Terms of Use Version 7.0, effective March 17, 2026).**

- *Liability-based entry caps (the most notable clause for advantage players):* "Underdog may limit the amount of entry fees that a user can pay in accordance with state-imposed deposit limits and/or based on liability risk to our business. Determination of unacceptable liability risk to us for the purpose of this Rule is at our discretion." (Section 5.iii) — This is the clearest explicit contractual basis among the three for limiting a winning/sharp player specifically because they represent risk.
- *Right to suspend/limit/terminate:* "Underdog shall be entitled to suspend, limit or terminate your account if we have reasonable grounds to believe (as determined in our sole discretion) that you are using the Services for an illegal or improper purpose."
- *Eligibility-based restriction/withholding:* Underdog "reserves the right, in its sole discretion, to terminate your Account, restrict your Account from accessing or participating in Fantasy Contests, withhold or revoke the awarding of any Fantasy Contest winnings … or limit your ability to withdraw."
- *"Advantage" language:* aimed narrowly at employees/operators of other DFS sites with access to "pre-release, non-public, and confidential user data" providing "an unfair advantage"; such entries are disqualified.
- *Promotional restriction by play pattern:* Underdog repeatedly reserves the right to "refuse or otherwise restrict your eligibility to participate in any promotion … based on your contest patterns or contest history."
- *Multi-accounting:* one account per person; violations allow suspension/termination and revocation of prizes.

**DraftKings Pick6 (DK Crown Holdings Inc.; Pick6 Terms of Use last updated July 19, 2024).** This is the most detailed of the three on prohibited-conduct grounds:

- *Refuse/limit entries:* "DraftKings may refuse to accept any Contest entry, any portion of a Contest entry, prohibit you from entering any Contest, or prohibit you from using the DraftKings Platform or the Services, in each case, for any reason or no reason, in DraftKings' sole and absolute discretion."
- *No vested right; suspend/terminate anytime:* "DraftKings may, in its sole and absolute discretion, for any reason or no reason, suspend or terminate your Account at any time."
- *Bots/automated play (explicit):* prohibits "any automated means, automated computerized software … including … bots, scripts, artificial intelligence, programs, parsers, spiders, screen scrapers, or tools … to automatically enter Contests or make Pick Set selections."
- *Syndicates/collusion (explicit):* prohibits "act[ing] collectively with or collud[ing] with one or more other individuals, including … working collectively to enter a series of Pick Sets for the same game."
- *"Advantage"/integrity:* bars users who accessed "pre-release information, confidential information, or any other information that is not publicly available" that "provides or could provide … an advantage," and anyone whose use "undermines the integrity" of a contest.
- *Suspicious volume/patterns:* if you enter "an inordinate amount of Contests … or … a series of Contests … in an unusual pattern," DraftKings may suspend the account, void entries, and withhold prizes.
- *Contest structure / entry mechanics:* each Pick6 contest carries a $1.00 entry fee; a chosen entry-fee amount is distributed across many $1 contests (e.g., a $50 entry = up to 50 contests). Each contest may have a "maximum number of entries per entrant." DraftKings "reserves the right to move you from Contest(s) to substantially similar Contest(s)" at its discretion (with a Connecticut carve-out requiring regulator approval). Reported per-contest cap is 150 entries.

**Bottom line on ToS:** All three have the contractual power to limit winners. Underdog's "liability risk to our business" clause is the most on-point authorization for limiting purely on the basis of profitability; DraftKings Pick6's terms are the most explicit on bots, syndicates, and advantage play; PrizePicks is the only one that names "entry limits" as a standing account tool but frames them around "legal, regulatory, responsible gaming, or other relevant purposes."

### 2. First-Hand Accounts of Being Limited or Restricted

The strongest first-hand evidence concerns **PrizePicks**, and it describes win-triggered account closures and withheld funds rather than the "silent max-entry demotion" of the popular threshold claim:

- **BBB complaint:** A user reported PrizePicks "withholding $6,061 in winnings … after closing it without providing a valid explanation"; the account "was suddenly closed or restricted without any prior warning," and withdrawal was denied. PrizePicks' reply cited ToS Section 9.1 (Deposits).
- **Trustpilot (verbatim):** "Prizepicks closed my account and seized all funds. They gave me no reason … they responded with the reason. They stated I used 18 different devices and deemed that excessive. I own 2 devices … I play by the rules and I win their money."
- **Trustpilot:** A self-described 65-year-old user: "they have been taking my losing bets for a year and now that I … started winning they find any reason to get you off … they only want losers."
- **Trustpilot:** An 81-year-old user reported a $170 balance frozen after the automated system "restricted" account login.
- **Aggregate:** BBB's 2025 Gambling and Gaming Study singled out SidePrize LLC (PrizePicks) for "almost 500 complaints in the last three years" for withdrawal problems, account terminations, and seizing of funds; PissedConsumer shows a 1.7-star rating across 436 reviews.

Trustpilot/BBB complaints are credible first-hand reports but are self-selected and unverified; they establish that PrizePicks does restrict/close accounts and that users widely *perceive* winning as the trigger, but they do not prove a mechanical win-rate rule.

For **Underdog** and **DraftKings Pick6**, no specific first-hand win-triggered limiting reports were located. Underdog BBB complaints exist but center on suspected multi-account/fraud triggers and "sole discretion" fund holds, not winning per se.

Verified context on how aggressively the *sportsbook* side of this industry limits (relevant as a benchmark, not to the DFS products themselves): at a Massachusetts Gaming Commission roundtable on limiting, Jack Andrews, co-founder of betting-education site Unabated.com, testified that "DraftKings had limited his account so severely that he could only bet $27 on a Super Bowl coin flip" (per Sportsboom's roundtable coverage). DraftKings, for its part, told the MGC that "less than just 1% of its entire user base was limited" (per Sports Betting Dime). This is the behavior pick'em apps are frequently contrasted against.

### 3. Can the ~55% / 200-Entry Threshold Be Corroborated?

**No.** The exact formulation traces to one affiliate/comparison page (xclsvmedia.com), which states PrizePicks "will reduce your max entry, refuse certain prop selections, and start 'demoting' your slips into Flex-only territory once your win rate crosses ~55% over 200+ entries." A targeted search of Reddit (r/sportsbook, r/dfsports, r/algobetting), rotogrinders, X/Twitter, and journalism found **no independent source** repeating these specific numbers. The only echoes ("max entry drops," "capped after winning") appear on other affiliate/AI-content marketing pages (sportbotai.com, propsbot.ai) that cite unlinkable, anonymized "Reddit users" and never reproduce the 55%/200 figures. PrizePicks' own Help Center and ToS contain no such mechanic. **Classification: unverified — treat as unconfirmed.** The claim is *plausible and directionally consistent* with the documented reality that these operators do limit consistent winners, but the precise threshold should not be relied upon or cited as fact in the Market_Betting build.

### 4. State-by-State Legal Status of the "vs.-the-house" Pick'em Model

The core legal issue: "vs.-the-house" pick'em (user picks over/under on player props against the operator) has been treated by numerous regulators as de facto unlicensed sports betting/parlays. Legal Sports Report has summarized that "more than a dozen states have taken action against certain DFS apps over the past year." Verified regulatory actions and responses:

- **New York:** In February 2024 PrizePicks agreed, in the NYSGC Stipulation of Settlement (signed Feb. 5, 2024), "to pay a settlement amount of $14,969,688 in the aggregate," covering revenue from purported contests June 4, 2019 through December 2023, due on or before March 1, 2024, and ceased paid contests. NY had approved rules in October 2023 banning prop-style fantasy. In October 2025 PrizePicks was awarded an interactive fantasy sports license and relaunched in NY (Feb 2026) with its peer-to-peer "Arena" product only. (Verified: NYSGC settlement, LSR, ESPN, Times Union.)
- **Florida:** Florida Gaming Control Commission sent cease-and-desist letters (first round Sept 2023, second with a March 1, 2024 deadline); PrizePicks, Underdog, and Betr exited. Both later returned with peer-to-peer products — Underdog "Pick 'Em Champions," PrizePicks "Arena." (Verified: LSR, The Capitolist, Stinson LLP.)
- **Michigan:** Adjusted consumer-protection rules bar contests that mimic prop betting; PrizePicks pulled paid pick'em. (Verified: LSR.)
- **Massachusetts:** AG sent cease-and-desist letters to 10 DFS operators; PrizePicks and Underdog had already switched to peer-to-peer, so they did NOT receive letters. PrizePicks launched "Arena" in MA (began March 8, per a PrizePicks spokesperson). (Verified: PlayMA, Straight to the Point.)
- **Other C&D / exit states documented in reporting:** Maryland, Ohio, Connecticut, Iowa, Tennessee (in the aggregate lists cited by multiple outlets), plus **Arkansas** (DFA C&D to PrizePicks and Underdog, Feb 2024, classifying them as sports betting), **Wyoming** (C&D to PrizePicks and Underdog), **Kansas** (six C&D letters; Underdog pulled in Nov), **Maine** (the Maine Gambling Control Unit issued a $391,850 penalty against Underdog in October 2023 — a $50 fine for each of 7,837 unique Maine pick'em customers, per LSR citing MGCU Executive Director Milton Champion; Underdog had accepted 482,771 wagers and ~$1.4M net revenue since January 2021 — and Underdog exited), **North Carolina** and **Mississippi** (Underdog exited ahead of/around sports-betting launches), and **Arizona** (banned pick'em Nov 2023; further C&Ds Feb 2026). California remains contested: PrizePicks and Underdog switched to peer-to-peer (Arena / Champions) ahead of an expected AG opinion; Underdog sued to try to stop the opinion.

**Peer-to-peer alternative products (confirmed current names/structure):**
- **PrizePicks "Arena":** peer-to-peer format where "prizes are drawn from pooled entry fees," replacing player-vs.-house; users are grouped by number of entries, entry-fee size, and experience level. Lower multipliers.
- **Underdog "Pick 'Em Champions" (a.k.a. "Champions"):** peer-to-peer tournament; users build a 2–8 athlete roster, earn "Champions Points," and payouts depend on rank within the field rather than fixed multipliers. Champions has its own per-sport and per-player entry limits plus a pending-entry cap.
- **DraftKings Pick6:** launched December 2023 as peer-to-peer from the outset, running on DraftKings' existing regulatory footprint, so it operates in many states where PrizePicks/Underdog had to pivot (it lists Void in NY and Ontario). This is the key structural distinction: Pick6 did not have to "pivot" because it was designed peer-to-peer.

Because state availability and product names change frequently, the Market_Betting build should treat any static state list as a snapshot and check each operator's live "where available" page before relying on it.

### 5. Differences Between the Three in Limiting Aggressiveness

To the extent this is verifiable rather than reputational:

- **PrizePicks:** The only platform with substantial first-hand evidence of adverse account action (closures, withheld withdrawals, fund seizure) — heavily represented in BBB/Trustpilot complaints, with users perceiving winning as the trigger. The specific "Flex-only demotion at 55%" mechanic is unverified. PrizePicks does explicitly reserve "entry limits" in its ToS.
- **Underdog:** Reputationally the most winner-tolerant ("rarely limits," per affiliate/industry commentary and claimed X screenshots of large unlimited balances). However, its ToS contains the single most explicit authorization to limit based on "liability risk to our business," so its tolerance is a business/policy choice, not a contractual constraint. Published caps include a $500 max entry and $750 max risk per player (per third-party guide Establish The Run).
- **DraftKings Pick6:** Industry writers (e.g., FantasyLabs) argue DK has "no reason to limit anyone" in Pick6 because it collects a fixed fee in a peer-to-peer pool regardless of who wins. Caveat: Pick6 shares ID/verification infrastructure with the DraftKings ecosystem, and affiliate commentary warns the same account-profiling that limits DK Sportsbook bettors *could* touch Pick6 — but no first-hand evidence of this was found. Treat the "won't limit" claim as plausible-but-unproven.

**Net assessment for a sharp/high-volume user:** On current evidence, Underdog and DraftKings Pick6 are lower-risk for account restriction than PrizePicks, but this is a reputational/structural inference, not a proven quantitative difference. The only hard, verifiable facts are the ToS powers (all three can limit at will) and the concentration of first-hand adverse-action complaints against PrizePicks.

## Recommendations

1. **Treat the 55%/200-entry Flex-demotion threshold as an unverified rumor in the Market_Betting model.** Do not hard-code it. If the build needs a limiting-risk parameter for PrizePicks, use a qualitative "elevated risk of account closure/withheld withdrawal once consistently net-positive," sourced to BBB/Trustpilot complaint patterns, not a numeric win-rate trigger.
2. **Stage the build by legal footprint first.** For each target state, pull the live "where available"/state pages for all three operators before assuming a product is offered; the peer-to-peer vs. vs.-the-house distinction changes payout math materially (Arena/Champions have lower multipliers and pool-based payouts).
3. **Model Underdog and DraftKings Pick6 as the primary "scalable" tracks and PrizePicks as "cash-out-frequently."** For PrizePicks specifically, assume withdrawal friction and possible account closure after sustained winning; withdraw balances promptly rather than accumulating.
4. **Instrument for the ToS triggers that ARE explicit:** avoid anything resembling multi-accounting, proxy play, automated/bot entry (explicitly banned by DraftKings Pick6 and effectively by all three), and collusion/syndicate patterns — these give the operators clean contractual grounds to seize funds.
5. **Benchmarks that would change the recommendation:** (a) a first-hand, corroborated report (with screenshots) of PrizePicks silently reducing max entry while keeping an account open would upgrade the threshold claim from "unconfirmed" toward "credible"; (b) any first-hand evidence of DraftKings Pick6 limiting winners would remove its "structurally safe" status; (c) new state legislation legalizing/relegalizing vs.-the-house pick'em would restore the higher-multiplier products in those states.

## Caveats

- **Source tiering:** ToS quotations are primary-source verified. State regulatory actions are corroborated by multiple reputable outlets (Legal Sports Report, ESPN, Times Union, PlayMA) and the NYSGC settlement document, and are treated as verified. First-hand limiting accounts (BBB, Trustpilot) are credible but self-selected and individually unverified. The 55%/200 threshold and the "Underdog never limits"/"Pick6 never limits" claims are affiliate/industry commentary and are flagged accordingly.
- **Affiliate bias:** Several comparison sites (xclsvmedia, propsbot, sportbotai) carry sign-up links and promo codes and have a financial incentive to shade platform presentation; their unsourced specifics were not treated as corroboration.
- **Volatility:** ToS versions, product names, multipliers, and state availability all change frequently. Every ToS cited here carries a specific "last updated" date; re-verify before relying on any clause or state status.
- **Complaint interpretation:** A user *perceiving* that they were closed "for winning" is not proof of the operator's actual reason; operators typically cite ToS sections (deposits, device/velocity checks, identity verification, multi-accounting). Both framings appear in the record and are reported here distinctly.
